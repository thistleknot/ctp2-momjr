    The Five Spheres

    The five spheres of magic form a wheel of opposition. Each has a natural enemy and a natural ally, creating a rock-paper-scissors dynamic that drives army composition and spell strategy.

                LIFE (White)
           /    \
    NATURE       SORCERY
    (Green)      (Blue)
           \    /
            \/
     CHAOS --- DEATH
     (Red)     (Black)

    Sphere Identities

    Sphere Theme Strength Weakness
    Life Protection, healing, angels Shields allies, purifies undead Low offense, slow
    Nature Growth, creatures, force Massive creatures, raw stats Vulnerable to control
    Sorcery Knowledge, illusion, control Dispel, counter, manipulation Fragile creatures
    Death Undead, drain, inevitability Ignores conventional defense Stopped by holy magic
    Chaos Fire, randomness, destruction Highest burst damage Self-destructive, unreliable

    Opposition Pairs

    Each sphere has a natural enemy whose units resist its signature spells:

    Sphere Natural Enemy Why
    Life Death Creation vs destruction; healing opposes decay
    Death Life Purification burns undead; light banishes shadow
    Nature Sorcery Raw force vs intellect; you can't outsmart a tidal wave
    Sorcery Nature Control vs primal chaos; structure crumbles against wild growth
    Chaos All (mild) Disorder disrupts everything, but nothing counters it fully

    Resistance Model

    Resistance is graduated, not absolute. There are no total blocks. Every spell has a chance to land — the question is how likely.

    Tier Resist % When
    Elite opposing 80% Target IS an elite opposing-sphere unit
    Aura (strong) 60% Co-located elite opposing protector
    Aura (moderate) 40% Co-located common opposing unit
    Chaos entropy 25% Target is a chaos creature (blanket, all spells)
    Hero self-save 35% Target is a named hero
    Lamp artifact +15% Additive bonus from the Lamp

    Maximum resistance: 95% (elite opposing + Lamp). Never 100%.

    What This Means for Gameplay

    Your army composition IS your spell resistance posture. Playing Death and facing Life creatures? Your kill spells are weakened against them — bring Chaos allies to punch through with fire instead. Playing Nature and Sorcery is controlling your creatures? Field stat monsters like Behemoth that shrug off illusions.

    This creates a scouting-and-countering metagame: see what your opponent fields, then adapt your army and spell selection.

    Sphere Advance Ladders

    Each sphere has a 6-rung research ladder that unlocks progressively stronger magic:

    Life Ladder

    Life Magic → Life Lore → Life Adept → Life Mage → Life Wizard → Life Master

    Nature Ladder

    Nature Magic → Nature Lore → Nature Adept → Nature Mage → Nature Wizard → Nature Master

    Sorcery Ladder

    Sorcery → Sorcerous Lore → Sorcery Adept → Sorcery Mage → Sorcery Wizard → Sorcery Master

    Death Ladder

    Death Magic → Death Lore → Death Adept → Death Mage → Death Wizard → Death Master

    Chaos Ladder

    Chaos Magic → Chaos Lore → Chaos Adept → Chaos Mage → Chaos Wizard → Chaos Master

    Higher rungs unlock: - Stronger summoned creatures - More powerful spells - Heroes (distributed across the ladder) - School multiplier bonuses to mana generation - Ultimately: the Cataclysm terrain transformation

    Growth and Stress Dynamics

    Each sphere has a growth direction (what it needs from allies) and a stress direction (what it becomes under pressure):

    Sphere Growth (needs) Stress (becomes)
    Life Nature's growth to sustain Rigid over-protection, turtling
    Nature Life's protection to survive Mindless creature spam
    Sorcery Death's inevitability to close games Paralyzed by too many options
    Death Chaos's aggression to break through Desperate over-extension
    Chaos Sorcery's direction to focus Self-destructive burnout
