# Inspirations — MoM Total Conversion

## The Color Wheel (MtG + Enneagram)

The five spheres form a wheel of opposition. Each sphere has a NATURAL ENEMY
(the thing that destroys it) and a NATURAL ALLY (the thing it complements).
This is the Enneagram's contraposition principle applied to game design:
each position's greatest weakness is the thing its opposite does effortlessly.

```
              LIFE (White)
             /    \
      NATURE       SORCERY
      (Green)      (Blue)
             \    /
              \/
       CHAOS --- DEATH
       (Red)     (Black)
```

### Enemy Pairs (immunity axis)

| Sphere | Natural Enemy | Why |
|--------|--------------|-----|
| Life | Death | Creation vs destruction; healing is the opposite of decay |
| Death | Life | Purification burns undead; light banishes shadow |
| Nature | Sorcery | Raw force vs intellect; you can't outsmart a tidal wave |
| Sorcery | Nature | Control vs primal chaos; structure crumbles against wild growth |
| Chaos | Sorcery (primary) | Disorder vs order; but chaos also disrupts itself |

### Sphere Identities

| Sphere | Theme | Strength | Weakness |
|--------|-------|----------|----------|
| Life | Protection, healing, angels | Shields allies, purifies undead | Low offense, slow |
| Nature | Growth, creatures, force | Massive creatures, raw stats | Vulnerable to control |
| Sorcery | Knowledge, illusion, control | Dispel, counter, manipulation | Fragile creatures |
| Death | Undead, drain, inevitability | Ignores conventional defense | Stopped cold by holy |
| Chaos | Fire, randomness, destruction | Highest burst damage, unpredictable | Self-destructive, unreliable |

### Resistance Principles

1. **Opposing sphere immunity.** Your natural enemy's creatures are IMMUNE to your
   signature spells. A Death Knight's Death Wish bounces off a Paladin. A Storm
   Drake's Stasis bounces off a Behemoth. This is absolute — no roll, no save.

2. **Aura protection.** Common units of the opposing sphere grant +30% resistance
   to co-located allies. A Guardian Spirit next to your army gives everyone a 30%
   chance to resist Death magic. This IS a roll — not guaranteed.

3. **Chaos entropy.** Chaos creatures have a flat 20% resistance to ALL targeted
   spells. Chaos doesn't have a specific counter — it's just inherently hard to
   pin down. But it also can't be protected the same way (no single aura helps).

4. **Heroes amplify.** Heroes of a sphere serve as mobile aura emitters for their
   sphere's protection type. Ariel (Life) protects against Death. Freya (Nature)
   protects against Sorcery. Tauron (Chaos) protects against Nature.

5. **Artifacts transcend sphere.** The Lamp grants flat +25% resistance regardless
   of sphere matchup. Future artifacts will be sphere-typed for specific protections.

### MtG Parallels

- Protection from [color] → our immunity to opposing sphere's spells
- Hexproof → our hero self-resistance (50%)
- Ward → our artifact protection (pay extra or it bounces)
- Indestructible → our undead-vs-death (can't kill what's already dead)
- First Strike → our positioning requirement (you must GET there first)

### Enneagram Dynamics

Each sphere's growth direction (what it NEEDS from its ally):
- Life needs Nature's growth to sustain itself (healing without growth stagnates)
- Nature needs Life's protection to survive long enough to reach full power
- Sorcery needs Death's inevitability to close games it controls
- Death needs Chaos's aggression to break through Life's shields
- Chaos needs Sorcery's direction to focus its power (unfocused chaos is just noise)

Each sphere's stress direction (what it becomes under pressure):
- Life under pressure becomes rigid (over-protection, turtling)
- Nature under pressure becomes mindless (all creatures, no strategy)
- Sorcery under pressure becomes paralyzed (too many options, never commits)
- Death under pressure becomes desperate (over-extends drain, goes insolvent)
- Chaos under pressure becomes self-destructive (friendly fire, burnout)

### Design Implication for Gameplay

The player's ARMY COMPOSITION is their spell resistance posture. If you're playing
Death and you see Life creatures incoming, you know your kill spells are useless —
you need your Chaos ally to break through with fire instead. If you're Nature and
Sorcery is controlling your creatures, you need raw stat monsters that can't be
dispelled (Behemoth doesn't care about your illusions).

This creates a meta-game of scouting and counter-building that mirrors MtG's
sideboard strategy: you see what your opponent is doing and adapt your army.

## The Chess Analogy (positioning as permission)

Three axes of the magic system make it chess-like:

1. **Piece identity** — which unit you have determines which spells are available
2. **Piece position** — range from caster to target gates whether the spell can fire
3. **Piece defense** — target composition determines whether the spell lands

A Death Knight approaching an unprotected city is a queen bearing down on a king.
Add a Paladin to the garrison and the Death Knight becomes a pawn — its signature
move is neutralized by the defender's positioning.

## The TRIZ Principles In Use

- **Asymmetry (P4):** Resistance isn't symmetric. Life resists Death, Death does NOT resist Life.
- **Counterweight (P8):** Each spell has a specific counter-unit, not a generic "magic shield."
- **Prior Action (P10):** Protection must be positioned BEFORE the spell lands.
- **Nesting (P7):** Auras nest — a Paladin protects the whole stack, not just itself.
- **Segmentation (P1):** Different protections for different schools. No universal shield.
- **Universality (P6):** The resistance check is ONE system with per-sphere parameters (not 5 separate systems).

## Game Inspirations

### Magic: The Gathering
- The color wheel: five philosophies in tension, each with natural enemies and allies
- Protection from [color] → our graduated resistance affinities
- Mana as a spatial resource (lands you tap) → our positioning-as-permission model
- Sideboard strategy (adapt to opponent) → our army-composition-as-spell-posture
- Creature keywords (flying, trample, hexproof) → our unit classification (undead, holy, chaos)
- Legendary creatures → our CantBuild hero scarcity

### Mage Knight (board game)
- The hand is the constraint: you can only do what your cards allow THIS turn
- Positioning on the map determines which sites you can assault
- Units as persistent resources that augment your base abilities
- Terrain matters: forests block ranged, mountains cost extra movement
- Night/day cycle changes what works → our cataclysm terrain transformation idea
- Solo puzzle of optimizing limited resources against a spatial challenge

### Lord of the Rings (thematic)
- The One Ring as a double-edged artifact → our Lamp (boon + bane, can't set it down)
- Nazgul as high-value pieces that project fear in an area → our aura system
- Gandalf can't just cast fireball everywhere — magic has COST and PRESENCE
- Armies of the dead can only be called once for one purpose → hero scarcity
- Terrain as narrative: Mordor IS death magic, Lothlorien IS life magic
- The Balrog as a cataclysm trigger: one creature changes the landscape

### Heroes of Might and Magic
- Town types produce sphere-specific creatures (like our tribe → unit gating)
- Heroes carry artifacts and level up, lead armies but don't fight alone
- Creature tiers within each faction (peasant → angel within Castle)
- Magic guilds in towns unlock spell tiers progressively → our rung ladder
- Wandering creatures guard resources on the map → potential MoM goodie hut equiv
- Split between adventure map (positioning) and combat (resolution)
- Morale/luck as probabilistic modifiers → our resistance percentages

### Cross-Cutting Themes

**Spatial magic** (all four sources):
- Magic isn't a menu you click from anywhere. It requires PRESENCE.
- MtG: tap lands where they are. Mage Knight: be adjacent to the target.
  HoMM: hero must be in the battle. LotR: Gandalf rides TO Helm's Deep.
- Our system: mage units must be physically positioned within range.

**Piece identity** (all four):
- Different units do different things. Not interchangeable.
- MtG: each creature has unique abilities. Mage Knight: each unit card is distinct.
  HoMM: archers vs cavalry vs flyers. LotR: each fellowship member has a role.
- Our system: unit-spell bindings give each elite a signature move.

**Terrain as faction identity** (MtG + HoMM + LotR):
- The landscape BELONGS to someone and reflects their power.
- MtG: swamps produce black mana. HoMM: each town type has its terrain.
  LotR: Mordor is literally death, Rivendell is literally life.
- Our cataclysm concept: researching endgame magic TRANSFORMS the land into
  your sphere's terrain, giving your creatures home-field advantage.

**Scarcity creates drama** (Mage Knight + LotR):
- The most powerful things are rare and hard to replace.
- Mage Knight: high-tier units are limited per game. LotR: the ring is ONE.
- Our system: heroes are CantBuild, summon-only, ladder-gated.

### Cataclysm Concept (from these inspirations)

The endgame isn't just "bigger armies." It's the world CHANGING.

When a player researches their sphere's Master advance:
- `Terraform()` transforms tiles in a radius around their cities
- Death: land becomes TERRAIN_DEAD (Ravenloft). Undead get combat bonuses there.
- Life: land becomes TERRAIN_SPECIAL1 (Elysian Fields). Life units heal/defend.
- Chaos: land becomes volcanic. Chaos creatures get attack bonuses.
- Nature: land becomes primal forest. Nature creatures move faster.
- Sorcery: land becomes crystalline/frozen. Sorcery units gain vision range.

The cataclysm is CAUSED by player action (research), not by a timer. It's the
consequence of pushing your sphere to its ultimate expression — and it permanently
changes the map for everyone. Other players see your territory transforming and
know what's coming.

`Terraform(location, terrain_index)` is base-verified (MagnificentSamurai scenario).
`TerrainType(location)` reads current terrain. Both are SLIC builtins.
Trigger: `HandleEvent(GrantAdvance)` on the sphere Master advance.

## The Dwarf Question (TRIZ + Six Hats Synthesis)

### The Contradiction
Dwarves are earth-aligned (Nature) but also industrial/technological (anti-Nature).
They're orderly (Life) but insular (not holy). They span multiple axes and don't
fit any single sphere cleanly.

### Resolution: Neutral Terrain-Gated

Dwarves are NOT a sphere. They're a RESOURCE — like iron deposits or mana nodes.
They exist where MOUNTAINS are. Any tribe that holds mountain territory and
researches the right advance can recruit them.

This matches:
- Original MoM: neutral race, recruitable by any wizard who conquered their cities
- Tolkien: independent, allied with no one permanently
- D&D: resistant to magic, earth-focused, their own civilization
- Gameplay: mountains become a strategic objective worth fighting over

### Implementation Path

1. Units: Dwarf Warrior (melee tank), Dwarf Crossbow (ranged), Dwarf Runesmith (magic-resistant)
2. Sphere: neutral (buildable by any tribe)
3. Advance prereqs: Iron Working / Masonry / Thaumaturgy
4. Terrain gate: mod_CanCityBuildUnit checks city terrain is hill/mountain
5. Resistance: Runesmith gets 50% base resist in the graduated affinity system

### Phase 2: Sphere Variants

Once recruited, dwarves can be UPGRADED by sphere-specific advances:
- Life → Holy Dwarf (healing aura)
- Nature → Stone Dwarf (earth elemental traits)
- Sorcery → Rune Dwarf (enchanted weapons)
- Death → Cursed Dwarf (Duergar, underdark)
- Chaos → Forge Dwarf (fire resistance, volcanic)

### What This Means for the Full Roster

The 5 spheres keep their core identities:
- Life = Humans, Angels, Paladins, Priests (holy order)
- Nature = Elves, Fey, Sprites, Treants, Beasts (wild court)
- Sorcery = Wizards, Mages, Golems, Elementals, Djinn (arcane enclave)
- Death = Undead, Skeletons, Liches, Wraiths, Vampires (necropolis)
- Chaos = Goblins, Orcs, Ogres, Demons, Drow (underworld)
- Neutral = Dwarves, Knights, Settlers, Iron Golem (available to all, advance-gated)

Dwarves sit alongside Knights and Catapults as the "anyone can have these if they
invest in the right tech" tier. The investment is terrain (hold mountains) + research
(mundane advances). No magic required — which IS the dwarven identity.

## v7+ Vision: Cross-Paradigm Feature Manifold

### The Insight

Each inspiration source contributes a LAYER of gameplay that can bleed into the
others. The question is not "which game do we copy" but "which FEATURES from each
game compose into a coherent system when layered?"

### The Sources (3D out → 2D → 1D)

**Layer 3 (deepest structural change — strategic):**
- MtG: Spell HAND (draw from pool, limited castable set per turn)
- HoMM: Hero stat system (Attack/Defense/Knowledge/Power modify outcomes)
- D&D: Ability scores that permeate everything (STR/DEX/CON/INT/WIS/CHA)

**Layer 2 (mechanical enrichment — tactical):**
- MtG: Cumulative enchantments (stack effects, combo synergy)
- Mage Knight: Tactical positioning on hex grid (adjacency = everything)
- HoMM: Creature morale/luck (probability modifiers from army composition)
- D&D: Feats (earned permanent bonuses that unlock new capabilities)

**Layer 1 (surface features — immediate gameplay):**
- MtG: Card draw economy (limited spells per turn, deck building)
- Mage Knight: Terrain movement costs (forest costs extra, roads are free)
- HoMM: Town creature tiers (tiered build queue, weekly recruitment caps)
- LotR: Fellowship mechanics (hero party bonuses for co-location)

### Cross-Paradigm Synthesis

#### The Spell Hand (MtG → CTP2)

Instead of the full spellbook being available at all times:
- Each turn, the player DRAWS N spells from their available pool (N = sphere rung)
- They can only CAST from what's in their hand
- Uncast spells persist (hand grows if you don't spend)
- Hand size cap = MomMagicMax / 50 (larger pool = larger hand)

**CTP2 Implementation:**
- `MomSpellHand[31][8]` — 8 spell slots per player (max hand size)
- `MomHandSize[31]` — current cards in hand
- BeginTurn handler draws a random spell from the sphere's available list
- Spellbook page only shows spells IN HAND (not the full catalog)
- Casting removes from hand (one-shot until redrawn)

**TRIZ:** The constraint (all spells available = no decision) is resolved by
SEGMENTATION (P1) — break the pool into a draw deck and a hand.

#### Hero Stats (D&D/HoMM → CTP2)

Heroes get 4 stats that modify gameplay:
- **Power** (INT) — increases mana generation (+N per turn per city with hero)
- **Command** (CHA) — increases experience gain for co-located units (faster veteran)
- **Fortitude** (CON) — increases magic resistance (hero resist tier goes up)
- **Arcana** (WIS) — increases spell hand draw rate (+1 card per N arcana)

**CTP2 Implementation:**
- `MomHeroStat[31][4]` — Power/Command/Fortitude/Arcana per player
- Stats increase with sphere rung research (each rung = +1 to one stat)
- BeginTurn handler checks hero co-location with cities/armies, applies bonuses
- No runtime unit stat modification needed — bonuses manifest as:
  - Power → directly adds to MomMagicPerTurn
  - Command → spawns a veteran unit alongside (proxy for XP boost)
  - Fortitude → adds to resistance threshold in the affinity system
  - Arcana → modifies hand draw count

**TRIZ:** The constraint (no SetAttack) is resolved by PARAMETER CHANGE (P35) —
stats don't modify the unit directly, they modify the SYSTEMS around it.

#### Cumulative Enchantments (MtG → CTP2)

Global/city enchantments STACK rather than being one-shot:
- Casting "Just Cause" the first time = 100 gold
- Casting it again while the first is "active" = 150 gold (compounding)
- Each recast costs more mana but gives more effect

**CTP2 Implementation:**
- `MomEnchantStack[31][10]` — count of times each enchant has been stacked
- Casting an already-stacked spell multiplies the effect by stack count
- Cap at 3 stacks per enchantment
- Dispel spells reduce enemy stacks by 1 (not remove entirely)

**TRIZ:** NESTING (P7) — the same spell cast multiple times creates layers.

#### Morale from Army Composition (HoMM → CTP2)

Army morale depends on unit DIVERSITY within a sphere:
- All same type = no bonus
- 3+ different types co-located = morale bonus (proxy: +1 to resistance checks)
- Cross-sphere units in same army = morale PENALTY (nature + death units don't mix)

**CTP2 Implementation:**
- The resistance check already scans co-located units (aura system)
- Add a DIVERSITY check: count distinct unit types at tgtLoc
- If diverse army (3+ types, same owner) → bonus 10% to resistance
- If cross-sphere army (units from 2+ spheres) → penalty 10% to resistance

**TRIZ:** COUNTERWEIGHT (P8) — diversity strengthens, incoherence weakens.

#### Fellowship Bonus (LotR → CTP2)

Heroes co-located with each other provide mutual bonuses:
- 1 hero alone = baseline
- 2 heroes together = +15% resistance for both
- 3 heroes together = +25% resistance for all (party synergy)

**CTP2 Implementation:**
- Already scan units at location in resistance check
- Count heroes in HERO_UNITS list at the same tile
- heroCount >= 2 → bonus 15 to threshold; >= 3 → bonus 25

**TRIZ:** MERGING (P5) — heroes together are more than the sum of parts.

### Priority Order (what to build first)

1. **Spell Hand** — fundamental game-changer, makes every turn a decision
2. **Hero Stats** — gives progression beyond just "unlock next rung"
3. **Fellowship Bonus** — small code change, big thematic payoff
4. **Army Morale** — already mostly implemented in resistance system
5. **Cumulative Enchantments** — needs stack tracking arrays

### Constraint Check

All of these use ONLY existing SLIC capabilities:
- int_t arrays for state tracking
- BeginTurn handlers for per-turn processing
- The existing targeting/resistance preamble for combat modifiers
- Random() for draw mechanics
- No new builtins required

The spell hand is the BIGGEST change because it fundamentally alters how the
spellbook UI works — instead of showing all spells, it shows only what you've drawn.
This means the generator would emit DYNAMIC page content (show/hide based on hand state),
which the current static alertbox system can't do.

**Resolution:** The "hand" is the RARITY you can access. Drawing a card = unlocking
a rarity tier for one turn. You always have Common. Each turn you "draw" and might
get Uncommon/Rare/Very Rare access for that turn only. Simpler, uses existing hub gating.
